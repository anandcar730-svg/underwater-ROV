Underwater Drone for Search in Shallow Waters

A low-cost remotely operated vehicle (ROV) that streams live video from rivers and ponds, carries its own light, and helps divers find lost items and recover drowning victims.

Why build it

Drowning recoveries often stall when a body is lodged underwater and the light fails. In one case a teenager's body was stuck beside a rock, and the recovery was paused for lack of light. A drone with its own lamp and camera lets rescuers keep looking from the bank.

Watch in real time

Live monitoring of shallow water with minimal turbulence.

Keep it affordable

Hobby-grade parts and a repurposed filter housing keep the cost within reach for local use.

Help the divers

Find lost items and locate drowned bodies so divers know where to go.

−2 mMechanical

Five thrusters, full control

Three vertical thrusters handle ascent, descent, stability and depth. Two horizontal thrusters drive forward, backward and sideways movement. The camera and lights sit at the front, and a tether leaves from the rear.

Hull

A water-filter housing, fitted with end caps and weights or floats to trim buoyancy, protects the electronics. Epoxy resin seals cables, connectors and sensor mounts.

Propulsion

3D-printed three-blade propellers, 70 mm across with a 60 mm pitch and a 10° twist, spin on A2212 1400 Kv brushless motors.

−5 mElectronics

How it works

The operator drives with a joystick connected to a ground-station computer. Commands travel down an Ethernet tether to a Raspberry Pi on the vehicle, which passes them to an Arduino. The Arduino sets each thruster speed through its electronic speed controller. The same tether carries the camera video back up.

The Pi also streams the camera feed and reads the pressure sensor. A 12 V battery pack feeds the ESCs directly, and a 5 V converter powers the Pi and Arduino.

Control protocol

The Pi sends one byte per command over a 9600 baud serial link. The Arduino answers with servo pulses to each ESC: 1500 µs stops a thruster, 1400 µs and 1600 µs drive it in either direction.

ByteActionThrusters
F / BForward / backBoth horizontal at 1400 / 1600 µs
L / RTurn left / rightHorizontal pair driven in opposite directions

−10 mResults

What it achieved

The drone reached its target depth of 10 m and moves at 15 cm/s. A full charge keeps it running for 40 minutes. In a flowing river the team saw slight turbulence from under-currents, and at night or in dark water the light gives about 1.5 m of visibility.

The Raspberry Pi and Python control programs worked together with the motors and camera, giving the operator real-time video and smooth control from the surface.
